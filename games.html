04 · Visual reference

Normal vs. Not-Normal

A quick-reference guide for what's typically common during a period, and what's worth telling an adult or doctor about. Print and keep somewhere handy: a locker, a bathroom, a nurse's office.

Usually common

What most people experience

  • •Mild to moderate cramping in the first day or two
  • •Bloating, breast tenderness, or mild headaches
  • •Feeling more tired than usual
  • •Mood changes in the days before a period
  • •Mild acne around the time of your period
  • •Cycles that aren't perfectly regular in the first year or two after your first period
  • •Cramps that ease up with rest, a heating pad, or over-the-counter pain relief
Worth telling someone

What's worth a conversation

  • !Pain so severe it stops you from going to school or doing normal activities
  • !Bleeding heavy enough to soak a pad or tampon in under an hour
  • !Pain during bowel movements or urination during your period
  • !Chronic pelvic pain that happens even outside of your period
  • !Repeated vomiting or fainting during your period
  • !Pain that doesn't improve with medication or rest
  • !Missing school every month because of period symptoms

Neither list is exhaustive, and this isn't a diagnosis tool. If something doesn't feel right to you, even if it's not listed here, that's reason enough to talk to a trusted adult, a school nurse, or a doctor. You know your own body better than any list does.
