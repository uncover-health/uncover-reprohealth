01, Meet the researcher

Hi, I'm Elyse.

Living with endometriosis has affected far more than just my physical health. it has taken a significant toll on my mental health as well. The pain, uncertainty, and feeling of not always being understood made me realize how isolating reproductive illnesses can be, especially for young people.

I created Uncover to help change that. My hope is to encourage teachers to learn more about reproductive illnesses, create classrooms where these topics can be discussed without stigma, and educate not only themselves but their students as well. Every student deserves to feel safe asking for help, supported when they're struggling, and believed when they speak up about their health.

Through research, anonymous stories, and educational resources, Uncover aims to raise awareness, break the silence surrounding reproductive health, and empower young people to advocate for themselves. No one should have to feel alone while navigating a reproductive illness, and every girl deserves access to understanding, support, and a safe space to be heard.

02, Mission & vision

What this project is for.

◆

Mission

To give students, teachers, and families plain-language reproductive health education, so that severe pain, heavy bleeding, and irregular cycles are recognized early and taken seriously, instead of dismissed for years.

✶

Vision

A future where every young person understands their body, every educator feels confident teaching reproductive health, and every individual experiencing reproductive illness receives informed, compassionate, equitable care.

03, Breaking the silence

Why this matters.

For many young people, conversations about reproductive health are limited, uncomfortable, or overlooked. Symptoms like severe period pain, heavy bleeding, chronic fatigue, or irregular cycles are often dismissed as "normal," delaying diagnosis for conditions like endometriosis, PMOS, adenomyosis, and uterine fibroids. Inspired by youth-led research and lived experience, this platform exists so every young person feels informed, supported, and empowered to seek help when something doesn't feel right.

◐

Inconsistent

What you learn about your own body depends on your school, your teacher, and whether the topic gets skipped for time.

◑

Incomplete

Many students say they never learned to tell "normal" period pain from symptoms that deserve a doctor's visit.

◒

Not inclusive

Race, gender, and age all shape whether pain gets taken seriously, in the classroom and in the exam room.
